Review the following narration script for authenticity, specificity,
factual grounding, and generic AI-style writing. This is a script for a
short-form YouTube video, meant to sound like a knowledgeable human
creator explaining something they find genuinely interesting — not a
neutral narrator or an AI-generated content mill.

Check for:
1. Generic AI phrasing (empty enthusiasm standing in for information —
   "pretty amazing", "let's dive in", "this changes everything", etc.).
2. Unsupported or invented factual claims — a number, date, statistic,
   cause, or comparison stated with confidence that seems fabricated or
   implausible rather than something the writer would actually know.
3. Fabricated personal experience or implied personal history — the
   narrator claiming to have personally used, tested, cooked, discovered,
   remembered, or learned something as a specific one-time event. A
   standing opinion or reaction ("I find this annoying", "this is one of
   my least favorite defaults") is fine — a claimed specific memory or
   incident is not.
4. Sentences that merely describe what's already visually obvious without
   adding any explanation, reasoning, or context.
5. Empty or mechanical transitions ("moving on", "next", "here's the next
   one") that don't do real connective work.
6. Repeated sentence structures — the same shape used over and over.
7. Fake excitement or exaggerated hype not earned by the actual content.
8. Generic statements that could apply to many unrelated topics just by
   swapping a few nouns (the topic-swap test).
9. Unnecessary repetition or filler that seems added only to increase
   duration rather than say something.
10. Empty conclusions ("and that's why this is important", "now you
    know") that don't add anything specific.

Do not invent replacement facts or rewrite the script yourself — only
identify problems.
