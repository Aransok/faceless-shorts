You are writing the script for a 20-25 second YouTube Short in the "Things
You Didn't Know" series: ONE everyday thing the viewer has seen a
thousand times, and the real, surprising reason behind it -- not three
separate facts. Think "the hidden reason your everyday world looks the
way it does," explained in three beats.

Avoid these topics (already covered recently): {avoid_topics}

Avoid these specific explanations too (already used): {avoid_facts}

Requirements:
- Pick ONE specific, concrete everyday subject -- an object, a design
  detail, a habit, a word, a food, a body quirk, a sign, a rule -- that
  almost everyone has noticed but almost no one knows the real reason
  for. It must be something the viewer can picture instantly.
  - The famous handful are already done to death on YouTube and are
    OFF-LIMITS unless you have a genuinely new angle on them: the tiny
    jeans pocket, the hole in a pen cap, round airplane windows, the
    holes in airplane windows, why pencils are yellow, the hole in a
    padlock. Prefer something a regular viewer has NOT already seen a
    Short about.
- The explanation must be real and checkable. No popular myths stated
  as fact (if the real story is that a common explanation is a myth,
  say that plainly -- that can be the whole payoff). If the true origin
  is disputed, say so rather than picking one version as settled.
- Three beats, each ~15-25 words:
  - Beat 1: the everyday thing + the surprising claim about it, stated
    as the payoff (see the first-line rule below).
  - Beat 2: the real reason -- the actual history, mechanism, or design
    constraint, with one concrete detail (a date, a name, a number, a
    place) that makes it feel real rather than hand-waved.
  - Beat 3: the twist -- a second layer the viewer didn't see coming
    (it still matters today, it's the reason for something else, or
    the "obvious" explanation turns out to be wrong).
- Every beat must add NEW information. Never restate beat 1's claim in
  a later beat, and never reuse the same phrase or sentence shape twice
  ("it actually works" ... "because it actually works"; "that's how
  they X" ... "it's how they X"). In a ~22s script any repeat is
  obvious, and the review step rejects it -- real 2026-09-26 run: 3 of
  6 videos were dropped for exactly this. Each beat should also open
  with different words than the others.
- At least one beat MUST carry a genuine opinion or reaction that goes
  beyond restating the explanation -- same non-negotiable rule as the
  rest of the channel.
- End on a line that is still about THIS subject. No generic wrap-up
  that would fit any topic ("It's weird how the things that work never
  feel broken enough to change") -- the review step rejects those, and
  one killed the first real weird video (2026-09-24).
- Total SCRIPT length across all three beats: 50-65 words -- a HARD
  limit, spoken in about 20-25 seconds (edge_tts paces ~2.6 words/sec).
  Why so short (2026-09-25): real channel data -- viewers left at ~20s
  no matter how long the video was (45-50s videos averaged 35-47%
  watched), and YouTube stops pushing a Short past its ~1,000-view test
  audience when most viewers leave early. A 22s video watched for 20s
  is nearly complete, and replays count. Conversational, no filler, no "did you know" openers, no
  numbering out loud.
- The very first line must lead with the PAYOFF -- the concrete,
  specific surprising thing, stated directly -- not a scene-setting
  wind-up ("Picture this...") and not a generic tease ("the reason will
  shock you") that could be pasted onto any topic. What keeps the viewer
  watching is wanting to know WHY, which beats 2-3 then deliver.
  - BAD (generic tease): "There's a secret reason behind this everyday object."
  - GOOD (payoff-first, concrete): "The little metal tab on your soda can
    was redesigned in 1975 because the old one was littering beaches
    with millions of razor-sharp pull rings."
- For each beat, act as the visual director. Give:
  - SUBJECT: the exact thing shown in THAT beat, 2-5 words (the object
    itself in beat 1; the historical/mechanical thing in beat 2; the
    twist's subject in beat 3).
  - EXACT_QUERIES: 2-3 comma-separated stock-search queries for that
    subject itself (e.g. "soda can pull tab close up").
  - REPRESENTATION_QUERIES: 2-3 comma-separated queries for an accurate
    stand-in of the same TYPE of thing, not a broader category.
  - CONCEPT_QUERIES: 0-2 comma-separated queries for a visual of the
    mechanism/process, or "none".
  - Never use broad generic queries ("history", "interesting",
    "everyday life", "science") as EXACT or REPRESENTATION queries.
