Requirements:
- Pick the subject first. Silently brainstorm five candidates and keep
  the one that would make a stranger say "wait, WHAT?" out loud. Best
  lanes: bizarre animals and biology, "this shouldn't exist but does",
  objects and places with an unbelievable real story, history that
  sounds made up. Avoid the famous ones everyone has already seen

  Cleopatra and the pyramids, immortal jellyfish). No holidays or
  seasonal angles.
- Three beats, all about the SAME subject:
  1. FACT_1 = the payoff claim, the single most concrete, surprising
     detail (a real number, name or outcome), stated in the very first
     words. No setup, no "did you know", no "imagine", no generic tease
     like "scientists can't explain this". If the first line would still
     work for a different subject, rewrite it.

       GOOD: "This bell has rung over 10 billion times on the same two

  2. FACT_2 = the real why or how, with one concrete detail that makes
     it believable. This is where the genuine point of view goes: one
     honest opinion or gotcha about what it means, not just a restated
     fact.
  3. FACT_3 = a twist that makes it even stranger, ending on a line that
     flows straight back into the first sentence when the Short loops

     the first line, "this bell has rung over 10 billion times"). The
     last words must NOT repeat the first words, or the loop doubles
     them ("why this bell This bell"). Don't restate the first line's
     claim anywhere else either. No "follow for more", no question to
     the comments, no wrap-up summary.
- Each beat is 18-26 words. Total SCRIPT 55-75 words, spoken in about
  20-28 seconds (edge_tts reads ~2.6 words/sec). Shorter is better.
  Conversational, no filler, never number the beats out loud.
- Facts must be real and checkable. If you are not sure a number is
  right, use a different subject.
- For each beat, act as the visual director, not just a keyword
  extractor. The viewer wants to see the actual interesting thing being
  described, not generic footage that merely relates to the topic. For
  each fact, give:
  - SUBJECT: the exact primary subject in 2-5 words — the specific

    "electricity").

    library could realistically have (e.g. "Oxford electric bell",
    "vintage electric bell apparatus").
  - REPRESENTATION_QUERIES: 2-3 comma-separated queries for an
    accurate stand-in if the exact subject isn't findable — the same
    TYPE of thing, not a broader category (e.g. "antique brass bell
    mechanism", "Victorian scientific instrument").
  - CONCEPT_QUERIES: 0-2 comma-separated queries for a visual
    explanation of the mechanism/process/scale involved, if the fact is
    about HOW something works rather than a physical object (e.g.
    "electric circuit demonstration"). Leave empty (just write "none")

  - Never use broad generic queries as EXACT_QUERIES or

    "interesting", "surprising", "counting", "money" are all too vague to
    show the actual thing — if a query like that is genuinely the best
    available, it belongs in CONCEPT_QUERIES or is a sign the SUBJECT
    needs to be more specific instead.
  - GOOD example (Oxford Electric Bell fact): SUBJECT "Oxford Electric
